# Phase 010 — Skeptical player challenge

Purpose: challenge the hook before technical construction rather than defend it reflexively.

## Q1 — “Isn't this just another tiny-board kingdom game?”

The product is not approved on the strength of board size or kingdom framing. Its differentiator is the **physical player-built Command graph**: cardinal ports, rotation, Load, priority tiers, switches, cut points, and Resonance. If a playable prototype is still described mainly by another game's signature interaction, Phase 009 forces an originality/pivot review.

## Q2 — “Why can't I just power everything?”

Because unlimited Command removes the game's central opportunity cost. The design promises competing valid uses of scarce Command, not arbitrary denial. Phase 030 must show that this creates interesting alternative plans rather than obvious toggling.

## Q3 — “Won't the network feel like doing electrical engineering homework?”

It must not. The simulation models connectivity, Load, priority, switches, cut points, and redundancy—not voltage/current/packet routing. The UI must state connected/powered/unpowered/isolated/reason state directly. If players need graph theory to make ordinary decisions, readability has failed.

## Q4 — “Is routing just busywork every Watch?”

It cannot be. Priorities and switches are reversible planning decisions, while routine operation is automated. The project must remove repetitive state flips whose correct answer is obvious. Phase 030 retry/comprehension thresholds test this directly.

## Q5 — “Will RNG decide whether I get the counter I need?”

Major threats are forecast. Random content is controlled by weighting, rerolls, banishes, visible unlocks, and deterministic seeded behavior. A run that rationally requires restart fishing violates the agency/fair-loss contract.

## Q6 — “Why should I accept a loss?”

Because the game must explain the causal chain from forecast and player choice to network/defense failure. If player explanation and authoritative root cause do not agree, the Phase 003/009 fair-loss gate fails.

## Q7 — “Won't one tiny board get repetitive?”

Replayability is expected to come from topology, priorities, doctrine rules, upgrades, pressure patterns, and risk—not multiple launch biomes or modes. If external evidence says content quantity is being used to hide a weak interaction model, Phase 009 requires redesign/cuts rather than scope growth.

## Q8 — “Are the four doctrines actually different?”

They must change structural rules and preferred routing/build patterns, not just percentage bonuses. Cross-doctrine content work is explicitly gated later; cosmetic doctrines fail the risk register.

## Q9 — “Why is combat automatic?”

Because the product tests preparation, not reaction micro. Siege converts a committed plan into readable consequences. Direct target spam or squad puppeteering would undermine the product pillar.

## Q10 — “Can I understand it with a controller or without color cues?”

That is a design requirement, not optional polish. Hover-only information, inaccessible focus paths, or color-only critical states are blocker-level failures at the owning gates.

## Q11 — “Does it need internet or an account?”

No. The core product is offline. Steam/platform services cannot become mandatory for simulation, saves, or progression.

## Q12 — “Why should it cost $14.99?”

$14.99 is only a working hypothesis from the commercial framing. The finished game's quality and current market must justify the actual launch price. The project must not reverse-engineer scope simply to defend a price hypothesis.

## Q13 — “What if Charterwake is not legally usable?”

The game must be rename-safe. Marketing title is not a save/content identifier. Public title remains provisional until formal/fuzzy/common-law review appropriate to commercial commitment.

## Q14 — “You haven't proven it is fun. Why GO?”

Phase 010 is a GO to **technical foundation and future prototype evidence**, not to launch. Preproduction has reduced design ambiguity enough that building the deterministic foundation is rational. Fun/replayability is intentionally tested at Phase 030 with precommitted kill criteria.

## Challenge conclusion

No skeptical question requires new launch scope to answer. The largest unresolved question—whether the core interaction is compelling—is assigned to a bounded prototype with a stop/pivot threshold rather than being answered by optimism.
