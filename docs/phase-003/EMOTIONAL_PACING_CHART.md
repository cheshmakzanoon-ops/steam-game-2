# Phase 003 — 15-Watch Emotional Pacing Chart

**Project:** Charterwake: The Last Relay  
**Status:** APPROVED PACING BASELINE  
**Date:** 2026-09-22

The bible fixes a standard run at **15 Watches across 3 Acts**, with boss assaults at Watches **5, 10 and 15**. This document defines the intended emotional and comprehension curve without inventing Phase 004's final numerical rules.

---

## Pacing principles

1. Every Act starts with enough breathing room to form or revise a plan.
2. Threat intensity rises faster than input burden.
3. Bosses test concepts taught before the boss.
4. Each boss is followed by a meaningful release, not prolonged housekeeping.
5. The late game should make the player feel powerful **and** exposed.
6. Novelty moves from "what is this?" to "what can I make this system do?"
7. A 35–50 minute target means no Watch exists solely as filler.

---

## Watch-by-Watch curve

| Watch | Act role | Intended pressure | Player-facing learning / test | Emotional target | Spectacle / relief rule | Failure-fairness requirement |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Act I orientation | Low | Board, Keep, Command scarcity, forecast, first priority tradeoff | Curiosity → agency | Small readable payoff after first commitment | No lethal surprise from an unexplained rule |
| **2** | First consequence | Low–moderate | Unpowered state and resource/defense opportunity cost | Productive tension | Resolution clearly reflects the first plan | Player can identify one decision they would change |
| **3** | First build identity | Moderate | Placement/rotation/network shape begins to matter | Discovery | First "I made that happen" synergy | Forecast is sufficient to plan; no counter requires unknown information |
| **4** | Boss forecast | Moderate–high | Prepare around a clearly previewed boss demand | Dread + planning confidence | Restrain spectacle; anticipation matters more | Boss-defining rule is known early enough to influence this Watch |
| **5** | **Boss 1** | High | Test the foundational loop: read → route → prioritize → commit | Tension → earned relief | First major impact frame / memorable resolution | Defeat must trace back to understandable early-system choices |
| **6** | Act II reset | Moderate | Repair/re-route and choose a stronger direction | Relief + renewed ambition | Fast reset; avoid reward-modal pileup | Recovery offers choices, not compulsory busywork |
| **7** | Specialization | Moderate | Doctrine/build identity becomes more structural | Ownership | Synergy presentation grows | Different viable priorities remain visible |
| **8** | Interdependence | Moderate–high | A strong network creates both leverage and vulnerability | Confidence + concern | Show network consequence clearly | No hidden rule silently invalidates a learned interaction |
| **9** | Boss 2 preparation | High | Reconfigure around a more disruptive future test | Focus + dread | Emphasize whole-board planning | Major boss interaction previewed before commitment |
| **10** | **Boss 2** | Very high | Test adaptation and network resilience, not just raw output | Stress → mastery/relief | Strong mid-run centerpiece | Loss chain identifies the structural failure, not just damage totals |
| **11** | Act III release | Moderate–high | Turn survival into a deliberate final build direction | Relief → determination | Quick recovery, visible power growth | Do not tax player with routine cleanup |

| **15** | **Final Boss** | Peak | Test the run's full topology, priorities, build identity and adaptation | Suspense → triumph or meaningful defeat | Largest spectacle, but causal readability remains mandatory | Victory/defeat must be reconstructable from known rules and visible state |

## Act I — Learn the language

### Desired player thought

> "I understand the board, I understand why Command is scarce, and I am already making choices I want to reconsider."

### Required beats

- first real tradeoff appears immediately;
- first resolution is short;
- first meaningful mistake is survivable enough to teach;
- Boss 1 is a synthesis of already introduced concepts.

### Act I anti-patterns

- tutorial text before meaningful interaction;
- harmless choices that pretend to be strategy;
- boss-specific gotcha introduced during Boss 1;
- too many content names before network concepts are understood.

---

## Act II — Own a strategy

### Desired player thought

> "This is becoming my network, and I can see both why it is strong and where it can break."

### Required beats

- doctrine/build identity becomes tangible;
- network redundancy and vulnerability become strategically relevant;
- pressure rises without multiplying routine actions;
- Boss 2 tests adaptation rather than simple stat accumulation.

### Act II anti-patterns

- only increasing enemy health;
- introducing disconnected subsystems;
- forcing a fixed build to counter the boss;
- drowning the board in effects that hide topology.

---

## Act III — Mastery under compression

### Desired player thought

> "My build is powerful. I know exactly what it does. I also know I cannot protect everything."

### Required beats

- mature build gets at least one satisfying payoff;
- final pressure makes priorities painful, not obscure;
- Watch 14 is a planning climax, not filler before a boss;
- Watch 15 resolves the entire run thesis.

### Act III anti-patterns

- housekeeping;
- surprise immunity/counter that invalidates a whole build without warning;
- final boss based on an unrelated minigame;
- victory animation so long that it dilutes the strategic payoff.

## Emotional intensity model

Use this as a qualitative test, not a fixed numeric difficulty curve:

- W1: 2/10
- W2: 3/10
- W3: 4/10
- W4: 6/10
- W5: 8/10
- W6: 4/10
- W7: 5/10
- W8: 6/10
- W9: 7/10
- W10: 9/10
- W11: 6/10
- W12: 7/10 with high positive spectacle
- W13: 8/10
- W14: 9/10 planning tension
- W15: 10/10 resolution

The curve is intentionally saw-toothed. Continuous escalation without relief makes every Watch feel the same.

---

## Pacing acceptance measurements for later playtests

Record, per run:

- time per Watch;
- planning time vs resolution time;
- number of meaningful state-changing inputs;
- number of routine/repetitive inputs;
- first point where the player can explain Command;
- first point where the player predicts a network consequence before the UI confirms it;
- moments the player reports confusion about cause/effect;
- whether Boss 1/2/3 rules were understood before commitment;
- whether post-boss cleanup felt meaningful or procedural;
- whether the player wants immediate retry and can state a different plan.

Do not optimize solely for lower time. The target is **dense decisions, not rushed decisions**.
